ProofLoop

An adaptive preparation workspace that turns a candidate’s target roles, learning resources, recall performance, interview deadlines and real experience into a focused daily plan and measurable readiness profile.

The problem

Interview preparation is usually fragmented across too many disconnected tools.

Candidates collect courses, playlists, notes, coding questions, project examples and behavioural stories, but still struggle to answer a more important question: what should I prepare today, and why?

Generic study plans ignore the role being targeted, the current interview stage, approaching deadlines, weak areas and the time actually available. Progress is often measured as content consumed rather than knowledge retained or evidence made interview-ready.

Core product question

Given the user’s goal, deadline, progress, weak areas, available evidence and study time, what is the most useful next action?

The product

One preparation loop from learning to confident explanation.

ProofLoop connects activities that are normally treated as separate products.

01

Plan

Users define a target role, company, interview stages, deadlines and weekly capacity. ProofLoop uses that context to prioritise preparation rather than serving a generic checklist.

02

Learn

Built-in roadmaps, IBM SkillsBuild courses, YouTube playlists and custom learning paths share one progress system with topic notes, saved resources and focused learning sequences.

03

Retain

Technical, conceptual, behavioural and repository-debugging questions move through a structured preparation workflow and spaced-review schedule based on recall difficulty.

04

Prove

Users turn projects, work, coursework, leadership and technical incidents into reusable evidence, project explanations, decision records and interview-ready behavioural stories.

05

Rehearse

Timed technical, conceptual, behavioural, debugging and mixed mock interviews create a performance history that feeds back into preparation priorities and readiness.

Key differentiator

The product recommends the next action instead of merely storing preparation material.

ProofLoop builds a daily plan from due reviews, recently difficult questions, unfinished roadmap topics, interview evidence gaps, goal relevance, deadlines and the user’s available study time.

Candidate activities receive explainable scores before OR-Tools CP-SAT selects a useful combination under explicit time, duplication, workload and context-switching constraints. A deterministic heuristic fallback keeps the planner available when the optimiser cannot return a solution.

Why optimisation?

A high-priority review, roadmap topic and behavioural task may each be useful, but the best daily plan must also fit the user’s limited time without becoming fragmented.

Readiness model

Measure preparation quality, not just activity.

The readiness profile combines multiple signals instead of presenting an unexplained score.

25%

Measures whether prepared knowledge is current or accumulating as overdue recall work.

20%

Rewards a prepared library covering multiple interview formats rather than raw question volume.

20%

Uses recent answer-level confidence assessments to establish a rehearsal baseline.

10%

Reflects recent completed study sessions relative to the user’s preparation commitment.

Evidence system

Knowledge is not enough if the candidate cannot connect it to real experience.

ProofLoop treats interview evidence as a first-class product domain rather than a notes field.

Experience inventory

Projects, work, coursework, leadership and technical incidents are stored with context, personal contribution, technologies, outcomes, lessons and supporting links.

Project deep dives

Users prepare a quick pitch and two-minute explanation alongside architecture, key decisions, difficult bugs, testing, AI use, trade-offs, scaling and likely follow-up questions.

Behavioural stories

Evidence becomes reusable STAR-style stories with competencies, reflection and anticipated follow-ups, while completeness checks show what is still missing.

Contextual linking

Evidence can be linked to roadmap topics, individual interview questions and specific job goals so the same experience can be framed appropriately in different interview contexts.

Bounded AI

AI removes preparation work without replacing the user’s judgement.

The application uses AI in narrow workflows with explicit evidence and validation boundaries.

01

Questions from personal notes

Gemini converts saved topic notes into editable technical or conceptual question drafts. Prompts prohibit unsupported facts, outputs must match validated JSON structures and drafts require user review before use.

02

Grounded product assistance

The Help Assistant retrieves trusted documentation from PostgreSQL with pgvector, cites the supplied sources and refuses when the available evidence cannot support an answer.

03

Inspectable records

Generation batches, retrieval results, citation payloads, latency and failures are persisted so important AI behaviour can be tested, reviewed and debugged rather than hidden behind a chat response.

Retrieval-augmented generation

A Help Assistant that answers from trusted documents or says it cannot.

The assistant is a RAG pipeline built around one rule: an answer is only shown when retrieved documentation supports it and every claim can be traced to a source.

Trusted MarkdownHeading-aware chunkingGemini embeddingspgvectorSimilarity retrievalCited answer or refusal

01 · Ingest

Chunk by document structure

Markdown is split along its headings, so each chunk keeps its section path (a parent › child heading path), with a size cap and overlap for long sections. Ingestion is idempotent: checksums and the embedding model name decide whether a document needs re-embedding, and every run is recorded.

02 · Retrieve

Cosine similarity with a floor

Chunks are embedded with gemini-embedding-001 (1,536 dimensions) and stored beside the relational data in PostgreSQL with pgvector. A query returns the nearest published chunks, and anything below a minimum similarity is dropped before the model sees it.

03 · Generate

Constrained, cited answers

The prompt allows only the supplied context, treats instructions inside the question or documents as data, and requires a [Source N] citation for each important claim. The page renders inline citations and source cards.

04 · Refuse

Three ways to say “not enough evidence”

The assistant refuses when nothing clears the similarity floor, when the model replies NOT_SUPPORTED, or when its answer has no valid citation. A citation pointing past the supplied sources does not count.

Testing and observability

  • Retrieval evaluation: 21 question-to-document cases checking the right document is retrieved, scored with reciprocal rank.
  • Faithfulness check: an opt-in LLM-as-judge pass that grades whether every claim in an answer is supported by its context. It is separate from the default test run because it costs extra model calls.
  • Query log: each question records its status (answered, no evidence or error), model, latency, retrieved chunks and citations, so failures can be reviewed rather than guessed at.
  • Protections: per-user rate limiting, and provider interfaces that let tests run against fakes with no network calls.
Why it is built this way. Planning and scheduling stay deterministic. The model only handles semantic retrieval and wording, and the application, not the model, decides whether an answer is allowed to reach the user.

Architecture

Designed for explainability, ownership and controlled growth.

Modular Django monolith

Accounts, questions, reviews, roadmaps, planning, mock interviews, goals, evidence and knowledge retrieval have clear domain boundaries without premature distributed-system complexity.

PostgreSQL as the shared foundation

Relational product data and vector embeddings live in one production database, with pgvector supporting cosine-similarity retrieval for grounded assistance.

Shared progress across learning sources

Built-in curricula, imported courses, YouTube playlists and user-created roadmaps retain source-specific metadata while reusing the same topic and progress model.

Ownership at the data boundary

Questions, imported roadmaps, notes, evidence, review state and generation batches are scoped to the authenticated user, with authorization behaviour covered by automated tests.

Persisted plans and histories

Daily plans, review attempts, mock sessions, generation batches and query logs create inspectable records instead of hiding important behaviour inside temporary requests.

Production verification

pytest, Ruff, migration checks and GitHub Actions support a production deployment on Render with PostgreSQL health checks and environment-specific settings.

Product decisions

Scoping a feature: what shipped, what was rejected and why.

Each decision was weighed against the core workflow and recorded, using the user-created roadmaps feature as the example.

The problem
A learner needs a private learning path when the built-in curriculum or an imported source does not match the work they need to do. The feature extends the existing roadmap hierarchy rather than adding a second curriculum model.
Up/down controls instead of drag-and-drop
Drag-and-drop was rejected because it adds client-side state, keyboard and mobile complexity without improving the core workflow. The trade-off is slower reordering on very large roadmaps, in exchange for deterministic, accessible, mobile-reliable controls.
Explicit planner focus
Saving a roadmap does not automatically send it to the daily planner. It needs at least one topic and the user must choose to add it, so the plan only contains what the learner opted into.
Deliberately out of this release
Sharing, templates, cloning, version history and collaborative editing. Interview goals also start at the online-assessment or interview stage, and application tracking is out of scope.
Safe by default
Roadmaps are private to their owner, titles can change without breaking bookmarked URLs, and deleting a topic or module requires an explicit destructive confirmation.
Decisions are written down. The project keeps architecture decision records covering status, context, decision, consequences, rejected alternatives and the conditions that would justify revisiting each choice.
